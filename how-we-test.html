How we test and choose picks

Clear criteria, verified links and a rubric we actually publish. UK-focused.

The shortlist: what a product must clear first

Before anything gets scored, it has to earn a place on the shortlist. Here's what every product has to clear:

  • Compatibility: does it work with your kit, and is it actually made for the UK? A charger with a folding EU plug or a hub that only shines on one laptop doesn't make the cut.
  • Specs vs marketing: we prioritise real standards - USB-C Power Delivery profiles and PPS, HDMI and DisplayPort versions, Thunderbolt and USB4 bandwidth - over headline claims. Plenty of "100W" chargers only deliver 100W on one port at a time; we read the spec sheet, not the box.
  • Reliability signals: warranty terms, brand track record and known failure modes. We stick to a shortlist of proven names with UK support (Anker, UGREEN, Belkin, ESR, INIU, Baseus, Cable Matters, Apple, Amazon Basics and similar).
  • Value: price-to-feature trade-offs, not just the cheapest option on the page.
  • Use-case fit: every pick names who it's for - travel, desk, gaming, multi-device households - and, just as usefully, who it isn't for.

Every link is verified against the live listing

The quiet failure mode of affiliate sites is a link that lands on the wrong product: a listing that was replaced, renamed or hijacked since the guide was written. We treat that as a hard error, so link verification is built into how this site publishes:

  • Before a guide goes live, every recommendation is checked against the live Amazon UK listing so the product title matches the exact product we describe - brand, wattage or capacity, and model.
  • Nothing ships stale. A guide cannot be updated unless every product link in it has passed that check recently; anything older than our freshness window gets re-verified first.
  • The whole catalogue is re-swept regularly with an automated check of every product link on the site. When a product is discontinued or its listing dies, we replace the pick and rewrite the guide around a verified alternative rather than leaving a dead recommendation up.

How we score (the SPP Score)

Every pick carries an SPP Score out of 10. This is our own editorial score. We work it out from a fixed rubric applied to each product's verified specifications and intended use. It's not an Amazon star rating, and it isn't taken from or matched to customer reviews.

The rubric scores four things from 1 to 10, and the scorecard sits under each pick so you can see the working:

  • Capability (35%): real output, capacity or bandwidth for the job. For chargers that means wattage; for power banks, capacity and output; for hubs and docks, port selection and data speeds; for cables and adapters, the link standard actually supported (a Thunderbolt 4 cable and a charge-only cable are different tools).
  • Versatility (25%): ports, features and compatibility for the use case - the things that let one product cover more situations.
  • Build and portability (20%): materials, form factor and travel-friendliness - GaN designs, foldable plugs, braided cables, brand build quality.
  • Value for its tier (20%): how well it fills its role as the overall, budget or premium pick. A budget pick only earns a top value score when it beats the overall pick on raw capability - "cheap" alone is not value. A premium pick is not fined for sitting at the top of the range either: it earns full value when its extra capability justifies the step up, and drops a notch when it does not.

Because every product here has already passed the shortlist, scores cluster towards the upper half of the scale; the weighted total (to one decimal place) and the scorecard are there to separate strong options within it, not to rate them against the whole market. The scores on a page are always distinct - at least 0.2 apart - so a comparison always ranks. We revise scores whenever we update a guide.

Scale change, September 2026. The headline score now reads on the shortlist scale: the rubric's weighted total is mapped so that a total of 6.0 shows as 8.0 and a perfect 10.0 shows as 9.8, one decimal, with the four sub-scores left exactly as the rubric produced them. Nothing scores a 10 - every pick carries a short What keeps it off a 10 line instead, and the honest lows stay visible in the scorecard and the cons. The reason is plain: a product that has already cleared the shortlist is a strong buy, and a headline 6.8 next to the word "best" told readers the opposite of what we meant. The raw rubric total is kept on file for every pick.

The overall pick is the benchmark. Our Best Overall pick is the one we would buy for most people, so its headline is the top of its page and nothing else on that page matches it. Where another pick's rubric total runs higher (a premium pick's usually does, because it is the more capable product), its headline is shown below the overall pick's and the uncapped total stays on file; the scorecard still shows exactly where that pick is stronger. There is one exception, and it is the honest one: a Best Budget pick that beats the overall pick on the Value sub-score is allowed to sit above it, because on value it is the better buy. When that happens it keeps the score the rubric gave it and clears the overall by at least 0.2, so the page still ranks and you can see why in the scorecard.

Distinct scores, September 2026. No two picks on a page share a headline or sit closer than 0.2, because a comparison whose numbers tie has not compared anything. The gaps are not a fixed ladder: each one is drawn from the real distance between the two rubric totals, at least 0.2 and at most 0.6, so a pick that is only just behind reads that way and a pick that is well behind does too. Where the two rules pull against each other, spacing wins over the 8.0 floor - a lower pick on a modest page can read 7.8 or 7.6. 7.5 is the normal low; a headline only drops into the 7.0s when the spacing genuinely needs the room, and it never goes below 7.0. No two guides carry the same set of scores either.

What we deliberately leave out

We don't display prices, star ratings, review counts or stock claims. Prices and availability change hourly and a stale number is worse than none; ratings belong to the retailer, not to us. Check the product page on Amazon UK via any of our links for the current price, availability and reviews - our job is the part that stays put: what the product actually is, and whether it's the right pick for you.

An honest note on hands-on testing

Not every item is physically tested in-house - no honest small site can claim that across a whole catalogue. Where we cannot test directly, we lean on documented specs, standards compliance, known platform quirks and each brand's reliability record - and we say so plainly rather than implying a lab we don't have.

The exceptions are marked. A product we own and use carries a Hands-on badge on the comparison table and beside its score, with a note on what our own use confirmed. Ownership changes at most two sub-scores - Versatility and Build, the two things use can actually prove - and the note says what moved and why. It never touches Capability (that is the spec) or Value (that is the tier rule), and it never adds a bonus on its own.
