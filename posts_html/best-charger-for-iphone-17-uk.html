FAQ

Q: Does the iPhone 17 need Apple's 40-watt charger? A: No. Apple quotes its 50%-in-20-minutes figure against a 40-watt adapter or higher, but Macworld's testing found an iPhone 17 drawing around 26 watts either way: 41% in twenty minutes on Apple's own Dynamic Power Adapter, and 42% on a 61-watt one. Every pick here clears what the phone was measured drawing.

Q: What charger does the iPhone Air need? A: Apple rates the Air from a 20-watt adapter upwards for 50% in half an hour, a gentler bar than the iPhone 17's. All three picks clear it, so choose on size and ports instead of wattage.

Q: What wattage do I need for MagSafe on an iPhone 17? A: Apple quotes 50% in 30 minutes from a 30-watt adapter or higher paired with its MagSafe Charger, and rates MagSafe and Qi2 wireless at up to 25 watts on the iPhone 17 and up to 20 watts on the Air. All three picks meet or clear that 30-watt bar, so the puck and the phone set the ceiling rather than the plug.

Q: Will a more powerful charger damage my iPhone? A: No. USB-C Power Delivery is a negotiation, so the phone asks for what it wants and the charger holds the rest in reserve. The cable is the part worth checking, since it can only pass what it is rated to carry, and our USB-C cable guide covers that end.

Q: Can I keep using my old iPhone plug? A: Yes, and it will still charge the phone; the lower its wattage, the longer that takes. What it won't do is reach the 20-minute figure on Apple's spec sheet, which is quoted against a 40-watt adapter or higher. One of the very small plugs from an older iPhone is the one genuinely worth replacing.
